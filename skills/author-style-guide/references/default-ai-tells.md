# Default AI-Tell Baseline

Include these as a default editorial checklist. They are prompts for scrutiny, not universal bans. The author may override any item.

- **Em-dashes:** Match the author's demonstrated preference. Flag repeated use when it creates a recognizable cadence rather than adding clarity.
- **Not-X-but-Y turns:** Use sparingly. State the claim directly when the contrast does not add real meaning.
- **Rule-of-three stacks:** Avoid repeated lists of three, especially when they are used for rhythm rather than substance.
- **One-line paragraph ladders:** Reserve isolated lines for pivots and conclusions. Keep explanatory material in real paragraphs.
- **Aphorism density:** Do not make every paragraph sound designed for a quote card or social carousel.
- **Generic intensifiers:** Remove empty language such as "transformative," "seamless," "powerful," or "game-changing" unless the author provides a concrete reason.
- **False certainty:** Distinguish fact, first-party observation, inference, and prediction.
- **Decorative headings:** Use headings to organize a real argument, not to create the appearance of structure.
- **Rhetorical questions:** Use only when the question advances the argument. Do not use them as a substitute for a claim.
- **Fake specificity:** Never invent anecdotes, metrics, sources, quotes, motivations, or outcomes.
- **Over-clean conclusions:** Avoid restating the entire argument in a neat slogan unless the author’s style genuinely calls for it.
