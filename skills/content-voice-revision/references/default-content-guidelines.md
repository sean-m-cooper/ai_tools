# Default Content Guidelines

Apply these only when target-repository guidance, an author style guide, and the source piece do not establish a different preference.

- Write with clear, practical authority. Ground claims in relevant experience, examples, or sources.
- Match paragraph length to the format and author. In long-form work, reserve one-line paragraphs for deliberate emphasis.
- Start from a specific event where possible. Then draw the principle and offer the framework.
- Preserve the author's punctuation and spelling conventions. Do not impose a house style that the author did not request.
- Avoid generic claims, repeated aphorisms, and repeated `not X but Y` turns.
- Use relevant numbers, dates, technologies, constraints, tradeoffs, and outcomes. Ask for supporting evidence instead of inventing it.
- Explain specialized concepts in terms appropriate to the intended reader.
- Date time-sensitive claims and distinguish fact, first-party observation, inference, and prediction.
- Use the repository's evidence or citation format. If none exists, choose a clear format suited to the publication channel.
- Follow channel conventions supplied by the author or publisher. Do not assume a standard call to action, link policy, or post structure.
